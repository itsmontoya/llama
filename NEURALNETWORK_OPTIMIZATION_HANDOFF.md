# Optimize the ARM64 single-row prepared FP32 projection

## Objective

Implement one upstream optimization in `github.com/itsmontoya/neuralnetwork`. Reduce single-row execution time for the existing packed right-transpose projection, using the real Llama 3.2 1B FFN shapes `M=1, K=2048, N=8192` and `M=1, K=8192, N=2048`. Preserve the released prepared layout and Llama's prefill gains. Validate both single-session and concurrent real Llama decode before publishing.

Use published `v0.10.20` as the control. Its tag resolves to `4f2860f264e2af392c0a27ced40f6df0435466a0`. The module checksum is `h1:kRDd694eMvtoWMoa6IP+qmhFrG2GgrtAP05sjOZXJLM=` and the go.mod checksum is `h1:SafbkuDehVErsuJAL5FHFsLhKJbeh/OI3ItvbmwDafw=`. ## Evidence and limiting factor

Measurements used an Apple M3 with four performance cores, four efficiency cores, 24 GB unified memory, Darwin/ARM64 Go 1.27.3, `GOARM64=v8.0`, `CGO_ENABLED=1`, `GOWORK=off`, empty `GOFLAGS` and default build tags.

- **Wall.** Gate/up plus down consumed 0.270 of 0.466 s in short decode and 1.230 of 2.101 s across 32 sustained tokens at P8. It consumed 4.116 of 7.019 caller-seconds in the two shared lanes and 7.600 of 12.885 in the independent lanes. These are about 58–59% of decode across all four scopes. In the sustained run the tied classifier consumed 0.402 s, K/V consumed 0.110 s, Q/O consumed 0.251 s and SwiGLU consumed 0.038 s.
- **CPU.** Phase-tagged Go samples attributed 0.21, 0.53, 4.69, and 12.10 sampled seconds to `rightTransposePackedTileARM64` in short, sustained, shared and independent decode. Dense one-row tile samples were 0.58, 2.48, 3.88, and 6.54 s respectively. CPU coverage varied, especially in the solo profile. Trace and projection boundary measures consistently put FFN execution on the critical path. In the annotated shared and independent decode disassembly, vector-load and lane-broadcast instruction addresses accounted for 4.63 of 4.69 and 11.97 of 12.10 sampled seconds inside the existing one-row loop. These addresses indicate sampling concentration. They do not establish stall causes. Primary prefill sampled 0.19 of 0.40 s in the packed tile. Keep the existing multi-row path as a guard.
- **Scheduler.** P8 decode created 19,168 projection helpers in the 32-step single session and 38,336 in each two-session run. Gate/up median creation-to-running increased from 30 μs sustained to 218 μs shared and 226 μs independent. Its running totals were 4.47, 9.01, and 21.04 s. Summed caller joins were 0.686, 3.690, and 7.092 s across all sites. Time blocked with helpers queued and none running was 0.012, 10.723, and 3.523 ms respectively. `WaitGroup.Done`/`Add` supplied the wake stack, with summed wake-to-running delay of 4.27, 14.70, and 15.70 ms. `pthread_cond_signal` was visible in CPU wake stacks. Queue and blocked wall can overlap executing helpers. Preserve the existing worker policy for this experiment.
- **Memory.** Uninstrumented P8 decode allocated 278.89 MiB per sustained run and about 557.7 MiB in either two-session run. Concurrent decode had zero GC cycles in the phase probe. The load-only probe allocated 9.53 GiB cumulatively and retained 4.604 GiB after forced GC. Two models retained 9.208 GiB. P8 primary peak RSS was 6.3735 GiB and median load was 1.850 s. Independent peak RSS was 11.221 GiB median.
- **Concurrency.** Three uninstrumented P8 observations had sustained throughput 15.11 [9.51–15.29] tok/s, shared aggregate 14.45 [11.43–17.98] and independent aggregate 9.46 [7.84–18.12]. Shared median lane rates were 7.26/7.23 tok/s and independent rates were 4.73/6.39 tok/s. The median faster/slower ratios were 1.010 shared and 1.019 independent, with one independent ratio of 1.63. At P6, sustained/shared/independent medians were 15.81/17.55/10.12 tok/s. At P4 they were 12.50/13.33/13.05. The P8 trace windows overlapped for 3.597 s shared and 7.421 s independent. Treat independent differences as noisy and retain the fast and slow observations.
- **Control.** Direct calls on real FFN weights with preallocated destinations took 2.309/2.156 ms for rotated gate/up/down at P1 and 0.790/0.800 ms at P8. Repeating the same weight at P8 took 0.741/0.732 ms. The tied classifier took 42.77 ms at P1, 13.04 ms at P6 and 13.45 ms at P8. Parallelism helps and costs queueing. The 64 MiB packed operands can remain expensive with or without rotation.

This is a mixed bottleneck. Work in the packed single-row FFN is the largest controllable wall-time component at the tested caps. Scheduler effects increase with two callers. The hardware traffic and stall counters were unavailable, so the data leave instruction throughput, cache behavior, and memory bandwidth unresolved. The kernel change below is a testable hypothesis. A roughly 10% saving in the measured packed FFN portion would correspond to about 6% lower whole sustained decode latency absent other effects.

## Call path and released implementation

Llama `(*Session).Decode` calls `(*Decoder).forwardBlock`, which uses `projectPrepared` in `tensor_ops.go` for gate/up and down. Both `LoadHFModel` and `NewDecoder` feed `(*Decoder).loadWeights` and `decoderWeight.store` into `tensor.PrepareRightTranspose`. Each of the 48 weights owns one FP32 representation. Preparation packs complete eight-output tiles in contraction-major order with a dense final tail. Llama allocates disjoint output via `projectionOutput` and calls `(*tensor.PreparedRightTranspose).GemmInto` with `TransposeB=true`, `Alpha=1`, and nil bias.

In neuralnetwork, `tensor.gemmInto` validates the shape, tensors, broadcast, aliasing and destination before dispatching to `internal/float32product.RightTransposePacked`. It then applies the existing alpha and bias epilogue. `RightTransposePacked` uses `rightTransposeOneRowWorkerCount` for M=1, partitions complete tiles, creates `workers-1` goroutines and processes the remaining range inline before `WaitGroup.Wait`. `rightTransposePackedRange` groups at most three rows, calls `rightTransposePackedTile` and `rightTransposePackedTileARM64` for complete tiles, uses `rightTransposePackedPartialTile` with bounded scratch for clipped ranges, and calls `rightTransposeRows` for a dense tail. In the ARM64 one-row tile, two vector accumulation chains cover eight outputs, and the disjoint output is written after the ascending K loop.

Attention K/V and Q use Llama `headProjection` → `project`, and O uses `project` directly. `(*Session).commit` → `(*Decoder).projectLogits` → `project` uses the tied dense classifier. Dense calls use `tensor.GemmInto` → `tensor.gemmInto` → `matrixProductValues` → `matrixProductInto` → `float32product.RightTranspose`. The one-row path can dispatch `rightTransposeOneRowParallel` → `rightTransposeOneRowTileARM64`. These dense paths are controls for the packed experiment.

## Proposed direction and alternatives

Test an ARM64 M=1 fast path that issues two adjacent existing eight-column tiles together within one worker-owned range. Share each left load and lane broadcast across both tiles and interleave independent vector accumulators while keeping each output's FP32 fused multiply-add sequence in increasing K order. Measure instruction and call overhead versus any cost from concurrent weight streams. Keep the same pack format, tile alignment, caller shard and join policy, epilogue and API. Retain the current tile for a leftover single tile and retain the current clipped-range, dense-tail, unsupported-shape, purego, race and multi-row paths.

Use the released single-tile path as the fallback if the two-tile experiment fails to improve Llama. Repacking would add startup risk and change memory ownership. A worker pool changes dispatch lifecycle and needs separate evidence of recoverable coordination time. The tied classifier needs its own weight layout and gather analysis. Keep each of those outside this pass.

## Correctness, ownership and concurrency

- Preserve FP32 operation order and results bit for bit on eligible inputs. Keep each accumulator initialized to positive zero with the same ordered FMA semantics. Exercise subnormals, signed zero, NaNs, infinities, epilogue coefficients and nontrivial bias where already supported.
- Preserve immutable prepared weights, input and destination alias checks, disjoint writes and call-owned scratch. Validate before writes and preserve the destination on errors. Preserve `Dense()` round trips, retry behavior and ownership of earlier returned logits. Check that workers do not outlive a call or record a scratch or operand pointer past its lifetime.
- Preserve bounds and synchronous cleanup with concurrent calls sharing a prepared weight and with independent models. Retain failure handling, session cancellation/cleanup semantics and per-lane fairness. Ensure uneven worker ranges and a clipped tile never read/write outside their shard.
- Preserve exact frozen digest and continuation behavior, cached/full-prefix parity, frozen Transformers reference logits and byte-identical CLI output with the existing tolerances. Preserve TinyStories and synthetic MHA/GQA/MQA traces. Run default, purego, race, and `go vet` upstream and in Llama.
